# Indie launch checklist, explained

This page goes through the same checklist as the [repository README](https://github.com/radarmakers-com/indie-launch-checklist), but gives one or two sentences of reasoning for each item, so you can decide what to skip. It is written for solo makers and very small SaaS teams that are about to show a product to the public for the first time.

The full version, with a printable PDF, lives on RadarMakers: [the RadarMakers launch checklist](https://radarmakers.com/launch-checklist).

## One week before

**Write the one sentence that says what the product does and for whom.** Most launch posts, directory entries and replies need this sentence. If you cannot say it aloud to a stranger in one breath, a visitor will not understand it from a headline either.

**Finish the signup flow as a stranger.** Use a private window and a fresh email address. You know where every button is, a new visitor does not, so write down each step where you hesitated and fix the worst three.

**Check the title, description and preview image.** Paste the link into a chat app and into a post draft. A broken preview image or a generic title is the cheapest problem to fix and one of the most visible.

**Test on the browsers and devices your first users use.** If your first users come from a developer forum, desktop Chrome and Firefox matter most. If they come from social media, test a phone first.

**Set up a way to receive replies.** A no reply address ends the conversation. People who write back after launch are your most useful source of feedback.

## Three days before

**Add a short demo.** A few screenshots or a short video that shows the product doing its job answers the question every visitor has: what does it look like when it works.

**State pricing plainly.** Say what the free plan includes and what it does not. Hidden limits are a common reason for angry first reviews.

**Write down your failure plan.** Decide in advance what you will do if the site is slow, a payment fails or your email provider stops delivering. It takes ten minutes now and saves a panicked hour later.

**Write text for each place you plan to post.** A post for a developer forum and a listing in a directory are read differently. Copying one text everywhere is easy to spot.

## Launch day

**Post, then stay.** Answering questions for the first few hours is part of the launch, not an extra. People judge a product partly by how its maker responds.

**Keep a list of questions and complaints, grouped by theme.** Three people asking the same thing in different words is one issue, not three.

**Thank the people who gave feedback and say what you changed.** It costs nothing and it is how a first handful of users turns into advocates.

## The week after

**Read the list again and fix the top three issues.** Do not fix everything, fix the ones that stop people from reaching the first useful moment.

**Email new signups with one specific question about their first use.** A single question gets more answers than a survey.

**Submit the product to a few directories where your users look.** Keep a simple record of which listings went live, because some take weeks and some never appear. You can submit a product to RadarMakers for free at [radarmakers.com/submit](https://radarmakers.com/submit).
